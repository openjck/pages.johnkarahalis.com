I run the wonderful board game
[Vantage](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/420033/vantage) for my game group.
As we have played, and as I have played alone, some questions about the rules
have come up. This page documents those questions along with their answers. It's
like our personal FAQ.

None of this is new, exactly. This page links to [the
rules](https://vantage.rulepop.com/), the [Stonemaier
FAQ](https://stonemaiergames.com/games/vantage/rules-faq/), [threads from
BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/420033/vantage/forums/66),
and things that the designer, Jamey, have said. Even still, I think it serves as
a good list of questions players may run into, even if they've already read the
rulebook and the FAQ, because some of these things are easy to forget.

While researching these answers, I found the glossary very, very helpful. Be
sure to read it in the _Book of Secrets_ or [on
Rulepop](https://vantage.rulepop.com/#find). I think Rulepop includes some
entries that were not included in the original printing of the _Book of
Secrets_, based on questions that came up after the game was first published.

Any narrative details that are not explained in the rule book have been marked
as spoilers. Just click on those areas to reveal the spoiler text.

## General guidance \{#general-guidance\}

### Universal Rule of Thematic Fun \{#urtf\}

Most importantly, remember the Universal Rule of Thematic Fun. The [official
Vantage rules](https://vantage.rulepop.com/#urtf) put it like this:

> If you are ever in doubt about a Vantage rule, card, ability, or anything
> else, **choose the most fun answer that makes sense thematically**.

I would go further. If looking up some rule or edge case would harm the flow and
fun of the game, don't worry about it. Just make a note to look it up later, and
consider enforcing the rule only in _future_ games. That's what I did, and those
questions became this FAQ.

Vantage is a cooperative game, so no one is really going to benefit at anyone
else's expense if a rule is misunderstood. At most, a misunderstood rule might
cause your game to finish more quickly than intended or last longer than
intended. That can affect replay value, though, so once you know the correct
rule, do consider enforcing it.

## Actions \{#actions\}

### Can a player perform a different location action during a later visit? \{#location-action-later-visit\}

Let's say a player performs a location action, leaves that location, and then
returns to that location later in the game. Can that player now perform
a _different_ location action at that location?

No. The [relevant rules](https://vantage.rulepop.com/#actions) state that
(emphasis mine):

> If you previously performed a location action here (**even in a turn some time
> before the last**), you cannot perform another. Choose a depart action or card
> action instead.

However, if the location card says that the action is "always available", then:

> This action is available even if you previously performed it or another
> location action here.

Think of it like this: if players _could_ perform multiple location actions for
a single location, many players would try every action before moving to another
location, which would be boring. If re-visiting a location _later_ were treated
any differently, some players would move back and forth between two locations to
perform all of the actions on those locations, which would also be boring.
I suspect that partly explains why it's prohibited. Limiting location actions
also increases replay value: if you want to try doing something else at that
location, well, you'll have to play again another time!

### When told to continue, can a player perform the same location action? \{#continue-same-location-action\}

Let's say a player performs a location action and the result of that action
either tells them to continue or tells them that they _may_ continue. Can they
perform the _same_ location action again, if it makes sense thematically?

No. The [relevant rules](https://vantage.rulepop.com/#continue) state that
(emphasis mine):

> If a location action’s result includes instructions to “continue”, you must
> perform an additional action, ignoring the restriction in step 1 (e.g., **if
> you already performed the engage location action, you may choose another
> location action, but not the same engage action**).

## Cards \{#cards\}

### In the card grid, do diagonal cards count as adjacent cards? \{#card-adjacency\}

No. The [relevant rules](https://vantage.rulepop.com/#grid) state that:

> Adjacency in Vantage always refers to orthogonal adjacency (cards that touch
> at edges left-to-right or top-to-bottom).

### Can players place dice on reserve or supply cards? \{#dice-reserve-supply\}

Reserve cards are cards belonging to a character that are not in that
character's grid, perhaps because there is no space in the grid for more cards.
Supply cards are horizontal cards, sometimes shared by the team, which often
involve goals. Can dice be played on these cards?

No. Stonemaier Games has addressed this in [their
FAQ](https://stonemaiergames.com/games/vantage/rules-faq/):

> You may only place challenge dice on cards in your grid (not in your reserve
> or supply).

### Some grid cards list an action at the bottom. What do the icons mean? \{#grid-action-icons\}

The [relevant rules](https://vantage.rulepop.com/#cost) state that:

> Some cards include the full cost, action, and result—do not reference the
> storybook for these actions. In addition to the type of action and the
> standard cost (the quantity of challenge dice to roll), some actions on grid
> cards also have a boost cost (a cube paid from that card).

The [Boost rules](https://vantage.rulepop.com/#boost) also state that:

> When an icon is used instead of the word “boost,” the number of icons
> represent the amount of boost. [One boost icon] is 1 boost, [two boost icons]
> is 2 boost.

In summary, a skill icon indicates that the action uses that skill. The number
is action's cost. The number of boost cubes shown are also part of the cost. For
example, if a _Help_ icon is shown, the number is 3, and 2 boost cubes are
shown, then it's a _Help_ action where 3 challenge dice must be rolled and
2 boost cubes must also be spent. Because it's a _Help_ action, the cost can be
paid down by using _Help_ skill tokens, and dice can be placed in any otherwise
valid dice slots related to _Help_ skills.

As explained in the text, _do not_ consult storybooks for these actions. It
bears repeating because it can be confusing. The result of the action is
described on the card itself.

### Some cards in the player grid show a number of boost cubes pointing to a skill icon. What does that mean? \{#card-boost-cubes-to-skill\}

It means that's one of the card's boost powers.  That number of boost cubes can
be paid _from that card_ to gain the indicated skill token. If you look at your
player card, you will notice that your player card also has a boost power like
this, allowing two boost cubes to be spent to gain one _Move_ skill token or one
coin. The iconography means the same thing when shown on another card. Just
remember to spend the boost cubes _from that card_.

### Can an action on the side of a grid card (e.g., _CONSUME_) or the action on the bottom of a grid card (e.g., _BREW_) be completed multiple times? \{#card-action-multiple-times\}

Yes, as long as the card remains in play and unless stated otherwise. Just
remember that each of those actions _counts_ as an action, so if you do that
action, you cannot do another action (like a location action or a move action)
on the same turn.

On BoardGameGeek, Jamey [stated
that](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3543862/article/46345323#46345323)
(emphasis mine):

> in general, card actions (**in your grid** and supply) can be performed
> multiple times; if not, they'll tell you otherwise. It's only location actions
> that have the restriction of 1 location action per location.

Note carefully that he said **supply** (horizontal cards, like missions and
destinies), not **reserve** (cards that could be moved into your grid at any
time). As the [relevant rules](https://vantage.rulepop.com/#grid) state
(emphasis mine):

> You may not use slots, **abilities**, or **powers** on **reserved** cards, but
> they retain boost tokens.

### Can the boost powers of grid cards be used any time you want? Can they be used multiple times? \{#card-boost-powers-multiple-times\}

Yes. Boost powers can be used as many times as you want, whenever you want. You
can even use the same boost power multiple times in a row.

This was discussed and clarified on [on
BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3591477/obelisk-question).

### When a card is removed from the grid, do the dice on it go to the challenge dice pool or the penalty dice box? \{#removed-card-dice-destination\}

They go to the challenge dice pool. The [relevant rules](https://vantage.rulepop.com/#grid) state that:

> Whenever you are instructed to replace or lose a card in your grid, refresh
> any dice on it and return any boost on it to the general supply.

Refreshing dice means putting dice back in the _challenge_ dice pool, so dice on
cards that are removed should be put in the challenge dice pool. There are
a couple of BoardGameGeek discussions about this:

* [Where do my dice go if I swap a card out of my grid?](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3545604/where-do-my-dice-go-if-i-swap-a-card-out-of-my-gri)
* [Placed challenge dice on removed cards](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3547830/placed-challenge-dice-on-removed-cards)

### When you replace an item card with a different but related item card (e.g., an item was upgraded to something better), what happens to the boost cubes and challenge dice? \{#replaced-card-boost-cubes-dice\}

It's a little strange thematically, in my opinion, but in this case, dice are
put back into the challenge dice pool and boost cubes are put back into the
boost cube supply. The [relevant rules](https://vantage.rulepop.com/#grid) state
that:

> Whenever you are instructed to **replace** or lose a card in your grid,
> refresh any dice on it and return any boost on it to the general supply.
> Return lost cards to the box. You must always have your character card in your
> grid. Insert the new card in the same position as the replaced card.

### When you replace an item card with a different but related item card (e.g., an item was upgraded to something better), can you put the new card anywhere in your grid? \{#replacement-card-location\}

No. It must be placed in the same location as the old card. The [relevant
rules](https://vantage.rulepop.com/#grid) state that (emphasis mine):

> Whenever you are instructed to **replace** or lose a card in your grid,
> refresh any dice on it and return any boost on it to the general supply.
> Return lost cards to the box. You must always have your character card in your
> grid. **Insert the new card in the same position as the replaced card**.

### When you move a card (let's pretend it's called "umbrella") from your grid to the reserve, does that card (umbrella) keep its boost tokens? \{#reserved-card-boost-tokens\}

Yes. This was [discussed on
BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3544781/cubes-on-cards-swapped-to-reserve),
and Jamey ruled that it makes the most thematic sense to keep the boost cubes on
the card (umbrella) that is being moved to the reserve.

Stonemaier Games now addresses this in [their
FAQ](https://stonemaiergames.com/games/vantage/rules-faq/) (emphasis mine):

> You may not use slots, abilities, or powers on reserved cards, but they
> **retain boost tokens**. When reserved, a card’s challenge dice are refreshed.

### When you gain a card, but there's no space for it in your grid _or_ reserve, what do you do? \{#gain-card-no-space\}

You can put the card in your reserve, or you can put the card in your grid and
then move a card from your grid to your reserve. Then, if you have too many
cards in your reserve, take one or more cards from your reserve and put them
back in the box until you _do not_ have too many cards in your reserve.

The [relevant rules](https://vantage.rulepop.com/#grid) state that (emphasis
mine):

> Whenever you gain a card but your grid is already full, you may either reserve
> the new card or reserve a card in your grid (other than your character card)
> to make space. **Then, if this exceeds your reserve capacity, lose an excess
> card from your reserve.**

## Movement \{#movement\}

### When do you resolve a one-cost move action to move? When do you pay some other cost to move? \{#moving-costs\}

On the compass of a location card, if the direction you want to move has
a number on it, you must resolve a one-cost move action. If the direction you
want to move has a star on it, you must look up the cost of the move in the
depart storybook.

Remember it like this: if there's a number on that direction, nothing else is
going to tell you how much the move costs. For that reason, you can be sure it's
the type of move that costs one move action. If there's a star on the card, it
_depends_. Think of it like an asterisk (*), which is used in writing to modify
a sentence with some caveat. The cost of that move _depends_.

### Why do some location cards suggest moving in some direction, even though it's hard to find what the card is talking about by following that direction? \{#unclear-direction-guidance\}

Stonemaier Games has addressed this in [their
FAQ](https://stonemaiergames.com/games/vantage/rules-faq/):

> You can gain a variety of quests (a type of goal) while exploring the Vantage
> planet, and you will often receive loose instructions to head in a specific
> direction. Please treat these directions similar to how you might in the real
> world–if someone says to head north, they mean “the area to the north”, not an
> unwavering path straight to the north. This is at least partially excuse to
> explore a new area, with completing the quest just one of many things you can
> do in that area–given the size of the world, I would recommend not fixating
> too much on any particular quest (though I understand it can be frustrating to
> look for something and not find it).

### How do you exit a cave? \{#exit-cave\}

Go backward/south, which is the bottom direction of the compass. Stonemaier
Games has addressed this in [their
FAQ](https://stonemaiergames.com/games/vantage/rules-faq/):

> When you’re in a cave and you move forward, you’re going deeper into the
> planet; backwards moves you closer to the surface. To enter most caves, you
> start with a cavern barrier and then move deeper to the next location;
> sometimes you can find loopholes to skip the barrier.

### Sometimes, the _Depart_ storybook mentions intercardinal directions (NE, SE, SW, NW). How does one move in those directions? \{#intercardinal-directions\}

A player can move in an intercardinal direction if they choose a depart action
for a given direction (e.g., west) and the _Depart_ storybook lists a direction
containing one component of the chosen direction (e.g., northwest or southwest).
The [relevant rules](https://vantage.rulepop.com/#cost) state that:

> If you move in a specific cardinal direction (e.g., move east) and there is
> a related intercardinal direction entry in the Depart storybook (e.g., move
> southeast), you may choose to move in that related direction instead of your
> original choice.

To ensure that these options are not forgotten, try to make a habit of reminding
_Depart_ storybook readers about this rule. For example, you might say, "I'll go
north, which is a depart action, but if northwest or northeast is an option,
I might want to do one of those instead."

## Player interaction \{#player-interaction\}

### Can one player spend coins for another? \{#sharing-coins\}

No. The [relevant rules](https://vantage.rulepop.com/#coins) state that:

> Unless you are at the same location as another player, you may not transfer
> coins between players.

This makes sense thematically. The other rules are written like players are
connected by radios, but they are not able to send material or even images to
each other.

## Text clarifications \{#text-clarifications\}

### When something tells you to put dice in the center, does it mean the challenge dice pool or the penalty section? \{#center-meaning\}

It's referring to the _penalty_ section of the center board. Stonemaier Games
has addressed this in [their
FAQ](https://stonemaiergames.com/games/vantage/rules-faq/):

> If a challenge dice ability says “place them in the center”, it’s referring to
> the penalty section of the center (otherwise it would say to “refresh” the
> dice, which returns them to the challenge dice pool).

### What are tokens? \{#tokens\}

Some cards and other text refer to tokens. Tokens are just boost cubes taken
from the supply.

The relevant [rules state](https://vantage.rulepop.com/#boost) that:

> Some cards request that players place ubiquitous “tokens” to track progress;
> boost tokens are used for this purpose.

[Other rules](https://vantage.rulepop.com/#tokens) state that:

> Vantage includes boost tokens (cubes) and skill tokens (cardboard). Cubes are
> sometimes used as counters other than boost (e.g., on goal cards) or as
> defined on certain puzzles.

It's not stated very clearly, in my opinion, but they are meant to be taken from
the **supply** as markers. Jamey clarified that [on
BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3541856/article/46345558#46345558).

### When a card refers to your "first time here," is it referring to your first time _this game_ or your first time ever, including previous games? \{#first-time-here\}

It's referring to the first time this game. Every game of Vantage is
a standalone experience, with nothing but knowledge carried over from one game
to the next. This is in keeping with that fact. This was discussed and clarified
[on
BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3629616/what-is-blood-vision).

### What counts as a [spoiler]? \{#define-area-1\}

What counts as a <Spoiler>city</Spoiler>? The [relevant
rules](https://vantage.rulepop.com/#terrain), which are marked as a spoiler,
state that (emphasis mine):

> <Spoiler>City locations are **vertical** cards labeled with the name of the
> city (or town, village, etc—they're all cities. The various horizontal
> locations in the capital are not city locations).</Spoiler>

If you're not sure, there's a pretty simple test: <Spoiler>is the location card
vertical, and does it look something like a city, maybe (but not always) with
<Spoiler>shops <Spoiler>and</Spoiler> sentients</Spoiler> walking around? If so, it's a city. Is it
horizontal, or is it vertical but obviously not a city (e.g., it's <Spoiler>the
inside of a sentient's house</Spoiler>)? Then it's not a city.</Spoiler>

### When is a [spoiler] an obstacle? \{#define-obstacle-1\}

When is a <Spoiler>sentient</Spoiler> an obstacle? Use your best judgement. The
[relevant rules](https://vantage.rulepop.com/#obstacles), which are marked as
a spoiler, state that:

> <Spoiler>The description of some actions include variations on the conditional
> statement “if the ____ is no longer an obstacle, reduce this cost to 1”,
> typically in regards to moving north past a creature in your way. Apply this
> at your discretion using the Universal Rule of Thematic Fun. For example, if
> you talked to a sentient at a location, you may not sneak past them using
> a “SNEAK past the sentient” action since they already know you’re
> there.</Spoiler>

In a game our group played, <Spoiler>I spoke to a sentient, and they were
friendly with me. After that, I took an action which costs 6 if they are an
obstacle or 1 if they are not an obstacle. Our group decided that they were
_not_ an obstacle because we spoke and they were friendly. After reading the
above, I think that was the right choice, especially in light of The Universal
Rule of Thematic Fun.</Spoiler>
